Annotated Bibliography

Backlinko. "Screen Time Statistics 2026: Everything You Need to Know." Backlinko, 2026, backlinko.com/screen-time-statistics. Accessed 13 May 2026.

This source provides data across different digital platforms to see how screen time is distributed amongst different users. It displays impactful information about how much people spend aimless time on their screens. I think that this would be a good source for my project because it is impactful data that shows how data that people may not be aware of themselves. This data will guide the user to show that the digital space I want to create for my project is not a bunch of algorithms to increase your screen time, but a space to be present and human.

University of Michigan Department of Psychology. "Time Flies By Faster As We Get Older. Here's Why." LSA: College of Literature, Science, and the Arts, University of Michigan, 19 Dec. 2022, lsa.umich.edu/psych/news-events/all-news/faculty-news/time-flies-by-faster-as-we-get-older--here-s-why-.html. Accessed 13 May 2026.

This source provides research and data about how we percieve time as we get older. It focuses on how our perception and experiences change in our mind as we age, and especially when life is more routine. This would be a valuable source for my project because it helps the user understand a feeling that they may be experiencing and shows how impactful it can be to live in the present moment and really experience time and life.

Harris, Tristan. "It's Time to Redesign the Attention Economy." Thrive Global, Medium, 10 Apr. 2017, medium.com/thrive-global/its-time-to-redesign-the-attention-economy-f9215a2210be. Accessed 13 May 2026.

This source addresses a real problem that is impacting our future as designers in this age where there is so much digital information and content available. It talks about how this problem is rooted in the economy, where it is not about human values, but metrics and numbers of how much time and engagement some content has recieved. This could be a great source to draw from because it highlights the need for a more human approach to design and digital spaces.
